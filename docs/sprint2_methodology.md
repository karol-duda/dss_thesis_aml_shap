## 1. Refined Research Questions & Translation to Modeling Task
The overarching objective is to quantify and mitigate the instability of SHAP explanations in tabular financial data. This translates into the following empirical tasks:

* **RQ1: How does extreme multicollinearity affect SHAP explanation stability?** *Modeling Task:* Train a baseline Gradient Boosted Decision Tree on the heavily correlated Vesta features. Calculate SHAP values across multiple training iterations with different random seeds to measure the variance in feature rankings.
* **RQ2: How does class imbalance impact stability?** *Modeling Task:* Compare stability metrics between an unweighted baseline model and a model trained with a weighted loss function (`scale_pos_weight`) designed to harden the decision boundary around the 3.5% minority fraud class.
* **RQ3: Can Seed Ensembling mitigate explanation volatility?** *Modeling Task:* Aggregate SHAP values from an ensemble of $N=10$ models and evaluate the statistical improvement in the explanation consensus.

---

## 2. Model Selection: Advanced GBDT Algorithms
XGBoost and LightGBM were selected over traditional statistical models or Deep Learning for three critical reasons:

* **Sparsity-Awareness:** The dataset exhibits massive "Informative Missingness" (e.g., `dist1` is 60% null). GBDTs natively handle `NaN` values via sparsity-aware split finding, avoiding imputation biases.
* **Non-Linear Interactions:** Fraud detection relies on complex combinations of temporal and categorical factors that tree architectures capture naturally.
* **Exact Explanability:** Tree-based models support TreeSHAP (Lundberg et al., 2020). Unlike model-agnostic approximation methods (like KernelSHAP), TreeSHAP calculates exact Shapley values. This guarantees that any observed instability stems directly from the data-model relationship, not from the explainer's sampling variance.

---

## 3. Literature Review & Key Take-aways
The methodology is grounded in the Rashomon Effect (Breiman, 2001), which posits that for complex datasets, multiple models can achieve identical predictive accuracy while relying on entirely different feature subsets. Recent XAI literature (e.g., Slack et al., 2020; Kelodjou et al., 2024) emphasizes that SHAP is highly vulnerable to this phenomenon under multicollinearity. 

* **Key Take-away:** Predictive robustness does not equal explanatory robustness. In AML compliance, an explanation that swaps causal features between training runs undermines system auditability.

---

## 4. Evaluation Metrics
Two distinct categories of metrics are utilized to separate predictive power from explanatory stability:

* **Predictive Performance:** PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under Curve). Given the drastic minority class (~3.5%), ROC-AUC is misleadingly optimistic because it rewards True Negatives. PR-AUC focuses exclusively on the accurate identification of the positive fraud class.
* **Explanatory Stability:** Jaccard Similarity Index and Spearman’s Rank Correlation. The Jaccard Index measures the overlap of the "Top 10 Most Important Features" across different model runs, effectively simulating a human analyst's workflow where only the top driving factors are investigated.

---

## 5. Baselines for Comparison
* **Predictive Baseline:** A standard LightGBM/XGBoost model trained solely on raw, unaggregated features. This will demonstrate the predictive lift gained by introducing the behavioral context features.
* **Explanatory Baseline:** The SHAP feature rankings generated by a single GBDT model trained on a fixed seed. The proposed mitigation strategy (Seed Ensembling) must significantly outperform this baseline in terms of rank consistency (Jaccard Index).

---

## 6. Validation Strategy & Data Splits
A rigorous Time-Series Split is utilized (First 75% temporal steps for training, last 25% for validation).

* **Rationale:** Random K-Fold cross-validation violates the strictly sequential nature of financial logs. Predicting past fraud using future learned behaviors constitutes Data Leakage (Look-Ahead Bias). The time-based split accurately simulates real-world production environments where models score incoming future transactions based on historical patterns.

---

## 7. Pre-processing Decisions
* **Entity Resolution (UID Generation):** Constructing behavioral aggregates (e.g., tracking a specific card's deviation from its mean transaction amount) to provide context to isolated transactions. The raw UID is subsequently dropped to prevent model overfitting.
* **Missing Values:** No imputation is performed. `NaN` values are retained to preserve the informative missingness signal.
* **Frequency Encoding:** High-cardinality categorical variables are frequency-encoded to preserve category prevalence signals without triggering the dimensionality explosion associated with One-Hot Encoding.

---

## 8. Methodological Assumptions
* **Assumption Check:** Standard linear models assume an absence of multicollinearity. The IEEE-CIS dataset massively violates this assumption (e.g., near-perfect correlation across hundreds of Vesta features). 
* **Model Justification:** While GBDTs do not mathematically require feature independence to make accurate predictions, their explanations (TreeSHAP) implicitly assume stable split selection. The dataset perfectly satisfies the conditions needed to empirically prove the failure of this assumption in high-stakes environments.

---

## 9. Strengths and Limitations of the Approach

**Strengths:**
* The GBDT + TreeSHAP architecture is highly optimized for sparse tabular data and eliminates explainer-induced approximation noise.
* The Entity Resolution methodology pushes the predictive baseline to SOTA levels, ensuring the stability analysis is conducted on a production-grade model.

**Limitations:**
* **Computational Overhead:** The mitigation strategy (Seed Ensembling with $N=10$) multiplies the training and explanation computational cost tenfold. While suitable for offline research, it introduces latency challenges for real-time inference pipelines.
* **Causal Ambiguity:** Ensembling stabilizes the attribution of importance across correlated features, but it cannot resolve the true underlying causal factor among perfectly correlated variables.
